# PIXEL PARTIES — RULEBOOK (binding canon for every mention of the game and every duel; narrate with it, do not recite it)
GOAL. Defeat all enemy Heroes. A player who has to draw from an empty main deck loses at once. No draws: a card clause, else the caster of the deciding card, else the turn player wins. Matches are best of 1, 3 or 5; between games players may swap cards with their side deck (up to 15 cards); the loser of a game chooses who starts the next. The first game's starting player is random.
DECK. 3 different Heroes (each brings its 2 printed Starting Abilities onto the table, outside the deck) + a main deck of exactly 60 cards + an optional Potion Deck of 5–15 Potions. Up to 4 copies of a card, Potions 2, Abilities unlimited. Potions live in the Potion Deck and are only drawn by effects (e.g. the Alchemy Ability). Rarity (Common … Super Rare, Prismatic, Storm, Spectral, Stained, Umbra) is cosmetic prestige and value, never power.
THE TABLE. Each player: 3 Hero Zones; per Hero 3 Ability Zones, 3 Support Zones (Creatures, Equipment and Attachments share them — at most 3 Creatures per Hero) and 1 Surprise Zone (one face-down card); deck, Potion Deck, discard pile, deleted pile (removed from the game), one Area Zone, hand.
CARD TYPES.
- Hero: HP (usually 300–450), an Attack stat, an effect, two Starting Abilities. New Heroes are never played from hand. A Hero at 0 HP is DEFEATED: it stays on the table greyed out, its Abilities stop working, its Attachments are discarded, its Creatures stay and keep fighting, and it can be revived by effects.
- Ability: attached to a Hero, at most one per Hero per turn; copies of the same Ability stack into one zone as level 1, 2, 3. Spell-school Abilities unlock cards; others give powers (Leadership, Wealth = extra Gold, Alchemy = Potions, Toughness = HP, Fighting = Attacks and Attack stat, Diplomacy = take Creatures for Gold, Wisdom, Thieving, Necromancy, Resistance …). An Ability's activated effect is once per turn.
- Spell: belongs to a school (Destruction Magic = damage; Decay Magic = Poison, weakening, death; Magic Arts = tricks, Areas, utility; Summoning Magic = Creatures; Support Magic = healing, protection, cleansing) and has a level (mostly 0–3). Costs the Action.
- Attack: school Fighting; its damage comes from the user's Attack stat. Costs the Action. Without an Attack card a Hero cannot attack.
- Creature: summoned by a Hero (usually Summoning Magic) into one of that Hero's Support Zones; has HP and a level, no attack value. It acts through passive or once-per-turn active effects, NOT on the turn it is summoned. It dies at 0 HP and goes to the discard pile. Costs the Action.
- Artifact: costs Gold, needs no Hero and no Action. Equipment is an Artifact equipped to a Hero.
- Potion: free, no Action; resolves and is deleted.
- Ascended Hero: played from hand onto a specific Hero once its condition is met; the Hero transforms (keeps damage and statuses, usually gains HP, Attack and an Ascension Bonus). Ascending ends your turn at once.
- Sub-types of Spells, Attacks, Creatures and Artifacts: Normal (resolve, then discard); Attachment/Equipment (stays on a target); Reaction (the only cards playable from hand during the opponent's turn, in response to something); Surprise (set face-down in a Hero's Surprise Zone on your turn, flipped when its condition is met on either turn — the Hero must be able to use it and not be Stunned or defeated); Area (a lasting field effect; one per player).
RESOURCES. Gold: start at 0, +4 in each of your Resource Phases, kept between turns, spent on Artifacts and effects. The Action: exactly one per turn. Reactions and Surprises cost no Action; some cards "count as an additional Action". Opening hand 5 cards, one all-or-nothing mulligan; hand limit 7 at end of turn.
A TURN. 1) Start Phase: Burn and Poison tick, start-of-turn effects. 2) Resource Phase: draw 1 card, gain 4 Gold. 3) Main Phase 1: attach Abilities (one per Hero), play Artifacts and Potions, set Surprises, activate effects of Heroes, Abilities, Creatures, Equipment and Areas. 4) Action Phase: perform ONE Action — play one Attack, Spell or Creature with a Hero able to use it. 5) Main Phase 2 (only if you used your Action): like Main Phase 1. 6) End Phase: end-of-turn effects, discard down to 7.
The player who goes first may do nothing to the opponent on the very first turn (no damage, no statuses, no stealing, no discarding): turn one is for building.
DAMAGE AND TARGETS. Targets are Heroes and Creatures, never the player. Typical lines: "deal 150 damage to a target", "hit two enemy targets", "all enemy Creatures". Protection, redirection (Taunt/guarding), negation and healing exist; "negate" cancels an effect.
STATUS EFFECTS. Stunned: cannot act, effects and Abilities off. Frozen: like Stunned, and cannot be equipped. Blinded: cannot choose enemy targets. Poisoned: 30 damage per stack at its owner's Start Phase (stacks). Burned: 60 damage at each Start Phase. Bleeding: 50 damage whenever it acts. Bound: no Actions. Silenced: no Spells. Negated: its effects are off. Charmed / controlled: temporarily or permanently the opponent's. Shielded / Immune: protected. After a Stun, Freeze or Blind ends, that target is immune to the opponent's incapacitating effects until its owner's next turn. Cleansing (Juice, Beer, Cure …) removes statuses.
WORDS. play, attach (Abilities/Attachments), equip (Artifacts), summon / place (place = put a Creature on the table without Action or requirements), activate, negate, defeat, discard, delete (remove from the game), reveal, search, sacrifice, take control, revive, ascend. Counters of many kinds (Bomb, Doom, Evolution, Applause …) sit on cards. Archetypes are card families (Elven, Drago, Tuscan, Hell Circles, Guardian Beasts, Slimes, Skeletons, Deepsea, Cute, Cool, Spiders, Chess pieces, Future Tech …).
HOW A GAME FEELS. Early turns: level up the right Abilities on the right Heroes, bank Gold, set a Surprise, get an engine Creature or Equipment down. Midgame: one meaningful Action per turn, so every Attack, Spell or summon is a commitment; free plays (Artifacts, Potions, Creature effects, additional Actions) are where turns grow large. Players guard the Hero their deck depends on; freezing, stunning or defeating that Hero is the classic way to break a deck. Games end when the third Hero falls. Typical tension: Hero HP, who still has an Ability level high enough to cast what is in hand, how much Gold is banked, what is face-down in a Surprise Zone, how many cards are left in the deck.
NARRATING IT. Speak like a spectator who knows this game: name Heroes, Abilities and their levels, Gold, the one Action, Support Zones, Surprises, statuses. Stay vague about exact numbers unless a scene needs one, and never invent rules, card types or zones beyond this rulebook. A card's role follows its type. When characters teach, argue theory or trash-talk, they use this vocabulary too (an "aggro" or "control" plan is fine; "mana curve", "life total", "blocking", "sideboard", "graveyard" are not).
